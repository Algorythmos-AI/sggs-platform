The [Hukamnama page](/en/hukamnama) shows the Hukamnama from Sri Harmandir Sahib, Amritsar, for
each day. This article sets out how a day gets there, so you can judge for yourself how much to
trust it. The short version: the text you read is never copied from anywhere. It is the same
verified text as the [Knowledge Base](/search), located by an exact match, and a day that cannot be
proven is not published.

## Where the day comes from

The Shiromani Gurdwara Parbandhak Committee (SGPC) publishes the Hukamnama each morning on
hs.sgpc.net, dated and with its Ang. Its main site, sgpc.net, shows the opening of the same
Hukamnama separately. Our service reads both pages politely: every minute from 05:00 to 09:00 in
Amritsar, then every ten minutes until 14:00. It never reads them more often than that.

Every date here is the Amritsar date. A page is used only when its own date stamp is today's date
in Amritsar.

## How a day is located

SGPC's page is used only to find *where* the Hukamnama is. Its words are compared with the lines of
our text around the Ang it names, and the day resolves only when one run of whole lines matches
**every word, in order**. There is no "close enough". The comparison ignores only what is not a
word:

- spaces, the dandas (॥ and ।) and verse numbers;
- invisible formatting characters;
- one character that SGPC's pages use for the subjoined ha (the sign U+0A51, where the printed
  Granth has a virama and ha), a known legacy of font conversion. Our own text never contains
  that character, so the mapping cannot join two different words.

The run must also end where a shabad ends (or, in a Vaar, where a pauri ends), so a page saved
half-way is not taken as complete. The page must then stay the same for two minutes. If sgpc.net
already shows today's opening, it must agree with the match, on the Ang and on its opening words.

The heading printed above the shabad (its raag, the Guru and the invocation, Ik Onkar) comes from
our text, never from SGPC's page.

## What is shown

What you read are our own lines, exactly as stored and cited by Ang. Transliteration is a separate
reading aid, and so is the English translation when it is shown. Neither is part of the Gurmukhi,
and both are labelled.

Each published day records two fingerprints: one for the whole text it was resolved in, and one
for the exact lines of the day (a SHA-256 hash). The website reads the day's lines again from its
own copy of the text and checks them against that hash before it renders the page, and the feeds
and the embed do the same. The public API's files are written only after the same check. If the
lines do not match, the day is not shown. The
[developers page](/en/developers#verify) explains how anyone using the API can make the same check.

## When a day cannot be proven

If any of these steps fails, the day is **held**: nothing is published for it, and the page goes on
showing the most recent Hukamnama under its own date, never as today's. A person then compares
SGPC's page with the text by hand. If the difference is on SGPC's side, the day is approved by hand
for the exact lines, and each approval is recorded. We would rather be late than wrong.

Every held or corrected day is listed in the
[corrections log](/en/learn/hukamnama-corrections-log).

## If something is wrong

If a published day turns out to be the wrong passage, or its lines are incomplete, it is corrected:
the day keeps its first publication time, gains a "Corrected on" note on its page and in the API,
and appears in the corrections log. To report an error, write to
[support](/en/support#contact). A person reads every report.
