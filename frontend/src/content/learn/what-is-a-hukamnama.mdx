A Hukamnama is the order of the day — a passage read from Sri Guru Granth Sahib Ji and received as
guidance for the moment. The word joins *hukam*, meaning command or will, with *nama*, a written
order. In a Gurdwara the Granthi opens the Granth at random after Ardaas, then reads the composition
found there, beginning from the top of the left-hand page so that a complete thought is read rather
than a fragment.

Sri Guru Granth Sahib Ji opens with the Mool Mantar, the statement placed before everything else in
the Granth:

The Mool Mantar stands at the very start of the Granth, before Japji Sahib and before the raags
begin. It is widely understood as the opening statement of the whole scripture. Reading a Hukamnama
is an old and widely kept Sikh practice; the description here follows the common Gurdwara custom.
This note is explanation, not a claim about the meaning of the line itself.
</Explanation>

## How Gurbani Soul draws a Hukam

In the app, the Hukam control opens the Granth for you and shows a *complete unit* — a whole shabad
or composition, not a single line lifted out of context. The text is the same verbatim Gurmukhi that
is stored in the corpus, cited by its Ang, so what you read on the device matches what is printed in
the source edition.

You can draw a Hukam at any time. Alongside the draw there is a **Hukam of the day**: one complete
Hukam for the whole day, chosen on your device by the date, which the widget shows on your Home
Screen and Siri opens when you ask for today's Hukam. It is not the Hukamnama issued from Sri
Harmandir Sahib. There is nothing to sign in to and nothing is sent anywhere: both happen entirely on
your device.

If you would like to search for a line you remember, or read around the passage a Hukam gives you,
the [Knowledge Base search](/search) and the [reader](/reader) let you move through the Granth Ang by
Ang. A Hukamnama is meant to be read slowly and reflected on; the app tries to stay quiet and simply
put the words in front of you.
