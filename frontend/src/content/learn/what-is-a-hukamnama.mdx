A Hukamnama is the order of the day — a passage read from Sri Guru Granth Sahib Ji and received as
guidance for the moment. The word joins *hukam*, meaning command or will, with *nama*, a written
order. In a Gurdwara the Granthi opens the Granth at random after Ardaas, then reads the composition
found there, beginning from the top of the left-hand page so that a complete thought is read rather
than a fragment.

Sri Guru Granth Sahib Ji opens with the Mool Mantar, the statement placed before everything else in
the Granth:

<Verse id={1} />

<Explanation>
The Mool Mantar stands at the very start of the Granth, before Japji Sahib and before the raags
begin. It is widely understood as the opening statement of the whole scripture. Reading a Hukamnama
is an old and widely kept Sikh practice; the description here follows the common Gurdwara custom.
This note is explanation, not a claim about the meaning of the line itself.
</Explanation>

Gurbani Soul has two different things with similar names. They are worth keeping apart.

## The Hukamnama from Sri Harmandir Sahib

Every morning a Hukamnama is taken at Sri Harmandir Sahib (the Golden Temple), Amritsar, and the
Shiromani Gurdwara Parbandhak Committee (SGPC) publishes it. This is the Hukamnama most people mean
when they speak of *today's Hukamnama*, and Sikhs around the world read it each day.

The [Hukamnama page](/en/hukamnama) on this site shows it, with a dated archive. We do not copy the
text from SGPC's page. We use their page to find where the Hukamnama sits in Sri Guru Granth Sahib
Ji, and then show the lines from our own verified text, cited by Ang. The match must be exact, word
for word. When it is not, nothing is published for that day until it has been checked by hand: late
rather than wrong. Until the day's Hukamnama is published, the page shows the most recent one under
its own date, and never calls it today's. The [developers page](/en/developers#verify) explains how
anyone can check the text they are given.

For the viakhya (explanation) and katha of the day's Hukamnama, go to SGPC at
[sgpc.net](https://sgpc.net/).

## The Hukam of the day in the app

The Gurbani Soul app has a Hukam control that opens the Granth for you and shows a *complete unit*:
a whole shabad or composition, never a single line lifted out of context. You can draw a Hukam
whenever you like.

Alongside the draw there is a **Hukam of the day**: one complete Hukam for the whole day, chosen on
your device by the date. The widget shows it on your Home Screen, and Siri opens it when you ask for
today's Hukam. **It is not the Hukamnama from Sri Harmandir Sahib.** It is a reading for the day,
chosen on your device, and nothing is fetched to make it. There is nothing to sign in to and nothing
is sent anywhere.

In both, the text is the verbatim Gurmukhi stored in the corpus and cited by its Ang, so what you
read matches what is printed in the source edition.

## Reading further

To find a line you remember, or to read around a passage, the [Knowledge Base search](/search) and
the [reader](/reader) let you move through the Granth Ang by Ang. A Hukamnama is meant to be read
slowly and reflected on; the site and the app try to stay quiet and simply put the words in front of
you.
